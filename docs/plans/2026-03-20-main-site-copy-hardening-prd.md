## 1. Purpose

The new site structure is materially better, but much of the current copy still reads like implementation scaffolding instead of finished customer-facing communication.

This PRD defines how to turn the rebuilt HaloAgency main site into a polished, conversion-oriented, SEO-capable content system.

The goal is not "rewrite everything for style." The goal is:

- to make every page sound customer-facing rather than architecture-facing
- to align copy with real buying questions and objections
- to standardize proof, FAQ, and CTA language across the site
- to prepare the site for page-by-page copy hardening and mobile/desktop review

## 3. Problem Statement

The current structure is right, but the copy still leaks internal planning vocabulary.

Examples of draft-like language currently visible on the site:

- "hub"
- "route"
- "main site"
- "trust layer"
- "proof layer"
- "service page"
- "intent"
- "scope"

These are useful internal planning terms. They are weak customer-facing terms.

The result:

- pages explain the site architecture instead of the business value
- some sections read like notes for the builder, not final copy for a buyer
- FAQs are partly good but still inconsistent in depth, purpose, and tone
- the new IA is stronger than the current messaging

## 4. Strategic Copy Decision

We are not changing the structure again.

We are translating the current structure into customer language.

That means:

1. Keep the page roles already established.
2. Replace internal IA language with buyer language.
3. Tighten each page around one audience problem and one decision.
4. Make proof and FAQ do real objection-handling work.
5. Run a separate content polish pass before visual QA is considered complete.

## 6. Copy Principles

### 6.1 Customer Language Over Architecture Language

Translate internal wording into buyer wording.

| Internal term | Customer-facing replacement |
| --- | --- |
| hub | overview page, service overview, just remove the label |
| route | next step, format, page, option |
| trust layer | pages that help a buyer verify credibility |
| proof layer | cases, results, examples |
| main site | website, company website, agency website |
| scope | volume of work, format of work, project outline |
| intent | what the person needs, what they are looking for |
| paid traffic | advertising, Google Ads, Meta Ads |

### 6.2 Concrete Outcome Over Abstract System Talk

Bad:

- "We build a connected growth system."

Better:

- "We help you get more qualified inquiries and see what actually drives them."

### 6.3 Commercial Clarity Over Agency Theatre

The tone should stay practical, direct, and calm.

Avoid:

- hype
- fake urgency
- internal jargon
- overexplaining website strategy inside customer copy

### 6.4 One Section = One Argument

Each section should do one job:

- show fit
- explain deliverables
- reduce risk
- prove outcome
- move to next step

### 6.5 Proof Must Be Specific

Whenever possible, proof should answer:

- for what type of business
- what changed
- what result improved
- why that matters

## 7. Page Family Strategy

## 7.1 Homepage

### Role

Position the agency, establish credibility, and route people into the right service path.

### Current copy issue

Too much of the homepage still explains the structure of the website itself.

### Copy objective

The homepage should sound like:

- "Here is what HaloAgency helps with"
- "Here is who it is for"
- "Here is the right path if you need a site, ads, tracking, or automation"
- "Here is why you can trust us"

### Must remove or reduce

- explanatory language about "main site"
- explanatory language about "service routes"
- explanatory language about the paid-layer architecture

### Must strengthen

- audience fit
- commercial outcomes
- differentiator
- fast proof
- clearer why/when to use the growth-plan form

## 7.2 Service Hub Pages: `/ads`, `/web`

### Role

Help visitors choose the right service direction before they go deeper.

### Current copy issue

These pages are structurally good, but some wording still tells the user what kind of page they are on instead of helping them make a buying decision.

### Copy objective

Each hub should answer:

- is this category relevant for me?
- which sub-option fits my business?
- what has to be true before this will work?
- where can I see proof?

### Must remove or reduce

- "this is a hub page"
- explanations about architecture mechanics

### Must strengthen

- fit vs non-fit
- selection logic
- channel/type comparison
- case-study cues

## 7.3 Service Detail Pages

Pages:

- `/ads/google-ads`
- `/ads/meta-ads`
- `/web/landing-pages`
- `/web/business-websites`
- `/web/ecommerce`
- `/tracking`
- `/automation`

### Role

Sell one service clearly to one type of high-intent visitor.

### Current copy issue

These are the closest to good sales pages, but some section descriptions still sound like notes for the strategist or builder.

### Copy objective

Each page should answer:

- when this service is the right choice
- what it includes
- what results it supports
- what is required to make it work
- why HaloAgency is a good choice for it

### Must remove or reduce

- phrases like "on this page"
- phrases like "on an indexable service page"
- self-referential explanations of content structure

### Must strengthen

- commercial fit
- buyer-specific pain
- timeline / scope clarity where appropriate
- page-specific FAQs

## 7.4 About

### Role

Reduce risk and make the team / operator feel credible.

### Current copy issue

The new About page is stronger than before, but it still talks too much in systems language.

### Copy objective

The About page should make a buyer think:

- these people understand business reality
- they can connect strategy and implementation
- they are practical, not theatrical

### Must strengthen

- credibility
- working model
- what kind of clients fit best
- why clients prefer one practical partner over fragmented vendors

## 7.5 Contact

### Role

Be the most natural place to start a real conversation.

### Current copy issue

Operationally good. Wording still references "main site" more than necessary.

### Copy objective

Make the page feel easy, low-friction, and businesslike.

### Must strengthen

- confidence around response expectations
- clarity on what to send
- reassurance for uncertain leads

## 7.6 Case Studies Index And Details

### Role

Translate proof into decision support.

### Current copy issue

Still some internal language around structure and proof.

### Copy objective

Cases should help people answer:

- have you solved something similar?
- for what kind of business?
- what result changed?
- what service path should I open next?

### Must strengthen

- business context
- service relevance
- "similar problem" framing

## 7.7 Packages

### Role

Anchor expectations around format, complexity, and budget.

### Current copy issue

The positioning is good, but some copy still feels defensive or internal.

### Copy objective

Help people understand:

- roughly what type of engagement fits
- what changes complexity and cost
- when to go to packages vs direct contact

## 8. FAQ System PRD

FAQ should no longer be treated as filler.

It should be a structured objection-handling system.

### 8.1 FAQ Rules

1. Each FAQ must answer a real pre-conversion question.
2. The first sentence should answer directly.
3. The rest should reduce doubt or add context.
4. Answers should usually stay under 70-90 words.
5. Do not duplicate the same generic FAQ across every page.
6. Each page family should own its own objections.
7. If a question belongs on a different page, move it there instead of repeating it.

### 8.2 FAQ Ownership By Page Family

Homepage FAQ:

- only broad trust / fit questions
- max 4-5

Ads pages FAQ:

- budget
- fit
- whether a site is needed
- Google vs Meta
- what happens if ads already run but results are weak

Web pages FAQ:

- timeline
- cost band
- whether current site can be reused
- whether SEO is included
- what is needed from the client

Tracking FAQ:

- GDPR
- access requirements
- what is included
- server-side necessity
- timing

Automation FAQ:

- complexity
- what can be automated
- whether it replaces staff
- integrations
- rollout format

Case studies:

- no FAQ unless a specific case needs one

Packages:

- only package selection / scope questions
- not broad service education

### 8.3 FAQ Content Standard

Each FAQ answer should follow this order:

1. Direct answer
2. Clarification
3. Next-step hint if useful

## 10. Mobile / Desktop Review PRD

Copy is not done after rewrite.

Every major page must pass a layout-aware content review on both mobile and desktop.

### 10.1 Desktop Review

Check:

- headline hierarchy is clear within one viewport
- proof is visible early enough
- cards do not feel text-heavy
- CTA hierarchy is obvious
- section order supports an evaluation flow

### 10.2 Mobile Review

Check:

- hero headline does not break awkwardly
- subheadline is still readable without wall-of-text density
- chips and badges do not wrap into noise
- cards do not become long text slabs
- FAQ answers remain scan-friendly
- primary CTA stays clear before and after the first scroll

### 10.3 Content-Specific Review Rules

Fail the review if:

- a section only makes sense to the team that built the site
- a buyer has to decode internal jargon
- cards contain too much text for mobile
- the page repeats ideas instead of moving the decision forward
- the CTA sounds generic or disconnected from the page promise

## 11. AI Workflow For Copy Passes

For Codex / CloudCode / future skill-assisted work, each copy task should be scoped like this:

### Required input

- route
- page role
- primary audience
- primary CTA
- proof sources to reuse
- top objections
- words to avoid
- source files to migrate from

### Required output

- rewritten hero
- rewritten section copy
- revised FAQ set
- CTA copy
- list of open factual questions if proof is missing

### Hard rule

Do not ask AI to "rewrite the whole site."

Do one route or one page family at a time.

## 12. Definition Of Done For Copy

A page is not copy-complete until:

1. It sounds customer-facing, not architecture-facing.
2. Internal planning language is removed or translated.
3. The page promise is obvious in the first screen.
4. The primary CTA matches the promise.
5. Proof is concrete and relevant.
6. FAQ handles real objections.
7. Mobile review passes.
8. Desktop review passes.
